Account Trading Scams & Safety Guide

The actual scam patterns found while researching 9 games' account marketplaces — not a generic warning list, but what's specifically documented per game.

Updated September 2026.

Why does account trading attract so many scams specifically?

Because the "product" being sold is a set of login credentials — once a buyer has them, nothing physically stops them from changing the password and locking the seller out, and nothing physically stops a seller from selling the same account to five different buyers before anyone notices. There's no delivery to intercept, no physical good to inspect. That structural weakness is why every single game we researched had at least one documented scam pattern specific to its own marketplace.

What is the single most common scam across all games?

Confirmed across Fortnite and Free Fire specifically:

Chargeback fraud. A buyer pays — often with a stolen card — receives the login, and disputes the charge with their bank weeks later. The payment reverses, but the seller has already handed over full account access. There is no game-side process to recover this, since the "theft" happened entirely on the payment side, outside the game's control.

What scam is specific to Roblox?

Fake "middleman" trade services and phishing sites that clone the Roblox login page to steal credentials, plus "free Robux generator" scams that request account access directly. Roblox's own Terms of Service ban account transfers entirely, so a scammed account has no recovery path — Roblox won't reverse an unauthorized transfer even for the victim.

What scam is specific to Brawl Stars and Clash Royale?

Fake escrow middlemen on Discord who take payment and simply disappear without delivering credentials, and "trial before you buy" setups where a seller gives temporary access, collects payment, then changes the password back and vanishes. Both work because there's no built-in protection in a direct peer-to-peer Discord deal.

What scam is specific to Mobile Legends?

Fake middlemen in Facebook/Discord groups, plus a "rent-to-buy" variant: a buyer is given temporary access, pays, and the seller then changes the linked email and locks them out before the sale is supposedly finalized.

What scam is specific to Genshin Impact?

Less a "scam" than a structural risk: HoYoverse's account security actively flags logins from new devices/locations after a transfer, and can permanently suspend the account — with essentially no appeal, since the linked payment history and ID verification make it nearly impossible to contest. This is the strictest enforcement of any game in our research, which is also part of why Genshin's account marketplace stays smaller than Fortnite's or Free Fire's.

What scam is specific to Minecraft's username market?

"Name-squatting" scams: a seller promises to release a short username on a specific date for a waiting buyer to grab, then either releases it early to someone else or not at all. Because Mojang/Microsoft doesn't officially support account transfers, there's no dispute process if this happens.

What actually protects a buyer or seller, if they trade anyway?

  • Escrow — a trusted third party (built into marketplaces like Eldorado.gg as "TrustShield") holds payment until the buyer confirms the account works, before releasing funds to the seller. This is the single biggest structural protection missing from most Discord-based deals.
  • Marketplace seller ratings/review history — a track record of completed sales is at least some signal, though it can be faked over time.
  • Never accepting "temporary access" as proof — this is exactly the setup both the "rent-to-buy" and "trial before you buy" scams above rely on.

None of this changes the underlying fact: trading still breaks every game's Terms of Service, and even a "successful," non-scammed trade can end in a permanent ban months later if the game detects it.

Is there any game covered here where trading is actually low-risk?

No. Every game's Terms of Service we checked (Roblox, Supercell's games, Garena's Free Fire, HoYoverse's Genshin Impact, Epic's Fortnite, Mojang's Minecraft, Moonton's Mobile Legends) explicitly prohibits account transfers for real money. "Low scam risk" and "against the rules" are two separate questions, and no game we researched clears both.

This isn't about trading — I just moved countries or got a new phone. Is my own account at risk?

It depends heavily on which game, and this is a different question from trading risk entirely — it's about a legitimate owner's own login pattern, not handing credentials to a stranger. What we actually found per game:

Real, documented risk:

Roblox — Roblox removed the manual country/region setting entirely; your account's region is now set automatically from your live IP address, and it ties into legal/compliance restrictions (some countries have been blocked outright). Genshin Impact — HoYoverse does not allow changing an existing account's server region at all; the only official option is starting a new account, and using a VPN to access a different region's server is a Terms of Service violation. Free Fire — Garena locks your account to whichever region it detects at creation (via IP, SIM, or app-store country) and provides no official transfer path between regions; unofficial transfer tools risk a ban, and Garena's own support explicitly recommends creating a new account for a new region instead.

Low risk, by design:

Clash of Clans, Clash Royale, and Brawl Stars — all three run on Supercell ID, which Supercell explicitly built and documents as the official, safe way to move an account between devices and countries. A new-device login on its own is not a risk signal here the way it is on the games above.

We did not find a specific, documented region/device-change risk pattern for Mobile Legends, Fortnite, or Minecraft in this research pass — that's a gap in what we checked, not a claim that no risk exists for those three.
