Our Research Methodology

Every price figure on this site traces back to a real, checkable listing. Here's exactly how we research, verify, and correct our data — including the mistakes we've already found and fixed.

Updated September 2026.

🌍 Research process compiled by AI across multiple marketplaces and countries, current as of September 12, 2026

Where does the price data actually come from?

We check live listing pages on active third-party marketplaces, sorted by price, rather than relying on old articles or forum claims. Our primary sources per game:

SourceWhat we use it for
Eldorado.ggLive listings sorted by price (highest and lowest) for Roblox, Fortnite, Genshin Impact, Clash Royale, Clash of Clans, Brawl Stars, Mobile Legends, Free Fire
igitems.comAggregate market-report pages showing price-bracket distributions with listing counts (e.g. "$0–$15: 57 of 1,147 listings")
MercadoLivre.com.brRegional cross-check for Brazil-heavy games (Free Fire)
TokopediaRegional cross-check for Indonesia-heavy games (Mobile Legends)

Minecraft is the exception: rare-username sales are documented through community reporting rather than a single marketplace, since that niche trades informally.

What's the "confirmed repeat" rule, and why does it matter?

We only publish a specific item, skin, or character as a genuine value driver if it appears in two or more independent top-price listings. A single mention doesn't make the cut — it gets flagged internally as unconfirmed and left out of anything we state as fact. This is why, for example, our Fortnite page names IKONIK specifically (it repeated across 4 of the top 10 listings we checked) while other skins we saw only once are not named as value drivers.

What do you do when you can't find any repeats at all?

We say so, instead of inventing an answer. For Brawl Stars, Clash of Clans, Clash Royale, and Free Fire, our top-10-by-price research found zero named-skin repeats — no single skin or cosmetic showed up twice across the highest-priced listings we checked. So those four pages state plainly that value is driven by stats (trophies, Town Hall/King Tower level, rank) rather than by owning a specific item, and don't name a "top skins" list that would have been guessed.

Have you ever published something wrong and then corrected it?

Yes, twice, both caught during this research pass and fixed rather than left standing:

  • An earlier draft of the Brawl Stars page named specific skins ("Star Shelly," "Challenger Colt," "Virus 8-Bit") as value drivers. Real listing data never confirmed any of them — the claim was removed and replaced with the stat-driven explanation above.
  • An earlier draft of the Clash Royale page made a generic claim about "rare and retired skins and emotes" driving value. Same issue — no repeat confirmation in real listings, same correction applied.

We're noting this here specifically so it's clear that "AI-researched" doesn't mean "never wrong" — it means checked against real data, and corrected in public when the data doesn't back up an earlier claim.

What counts as a mistake we've already made, that you fixed?

Beyond the two skin-list corrections above: our first pass at price ranges for all 9 games started at roughly $100 as a floor. Checking live listings directly showed real prices go far lower — several games have confirmed listings under $1, with true floors near zero for low-progress accounts. All 9 game pages were rewritten with corrected floors after this was caught.

How often is this data refreshed, and what does the freshness badge mean?

The badge under each page's headline states the date that page's data was last checked against live listings. It is not a permanent guarantee — marketplace prices move — but it tells you exactly how old the numbers you're reading are, rather than leaving you to guess whether a "top accounts" claim is from this month or three years ago (the Fortnite comparison on our cross-game comparison page shows exactly why that distinction matters).

What don't you do, even though it would be easier?

We don't extrapolate a game's typical price range from a handful of high-end listings, don't reuse a price found for one region as if it applies globally without a regional cross-check, and don't carry forward a number from an earlier version of this site without re-verifying it against a current listing first.
