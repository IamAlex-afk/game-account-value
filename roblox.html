🟦 Roblox Account Value

There is no verified public market for Roblox accounts — trades happen off-platform and prices are never disclosed. What follows is what actually gets documented: collector holdings, item economics, and the risks of trading an account at all.

How much is a Roblox account actually worth?

There is no verified record of a single Roblox account being sold for a specific price — account trading itself violates Roblox's Terms of Service, so transactions (when they happen) occur off-platform with no public price disclosure. What is documented instead is the in-game net worth of top collectors, measured in the value of their Limited items. That is a different thing from a resale price, and this page treats it that way.

Where do people even claim to buy and sell Roblox accounts?

Unlike the other games in this report, Roblox does not have an established third-party resale marketplace with visible listings and price history. Item and currency trading happens inside Roblox's own (regulated) trade system; account-level sales mostly happen through private Discord/forum deals that leave no public price trail — which is also why they carry the highest scam risk of any game covered here.

What is the most expensive Roblox account ever recorded?

Reported holdings, not a sale

Roblox Den's richest-players ranking places Simoon68 at #1, with a Limited items collection estimated around 700,000,000 R$ (~$2.45M) — the highest figure ever recorded for a real player, not a bot or corporate account. This is a valuation of held items, not a sale price; nobody has documented actually buying this account for that amount.

What actually determines a Roblox account's value?

  • Discontinued Limited items — rare cosmetics no longer sold; some individually resell (via Roblox's own trade system) for thousands of dollars in Robux value
  • Account age and OG status — usernames and registration dates from before 2010 carry collector cachet
  • Robux balance — liquid in-game currency
  • Premium membership history — longer subscription history signals a real, aged account rather than a farmed one
  • Username quality — short, dictionary-word, or number-free usernames are independently desirable
  • Game-pass and developer product ownership across popular experiences

How can I make my own account more valuable (without trading it)?

  • Hold onto Limited items rather than trading them away for short-term Robux — discontinued items only get scarcer
  • Keep Premium membership active continuously rather than letting it lapse and restart
  • Diversify across a few different Limiteds rather than concentrating value in one item, since Roblox has occasionally delisted or devalued individual items
  • Treat this as a collecting hobby with real risk, not an investment — Limited item values are illiquid and can swing hard

What are the biggest scams around Roblox accounts and items?

Account and item trading scams are extremely common in the Roblox community: fake "middleman" trade services, phishing sites that clone the Roblox login page to steal credentials, and "free Robux generator" scams that request account access. Because Roblox's own Terms of Service prohibit account transfers, victims of a trade scam have no recourse — Roblox will not restore a scammed account or reverse an item trade, and the scammed account itself remains banned if Roblox detects the unauthorized transfer.

Is it against the rules to buy or sell a Roblox account?

Yes. Roblox's Terms of Service explicitly prohibit transferring account ownership and trading accounts for real-world money. Accounts involved in detected transfers can be permanently suspended, with no appeal process for the buyer or seller.

Why doesn't Roblox have a real account marketplace like other games on this list?

Roblox enforces its no-account-trading policy more visibly than most of the other games covered in this report, and its economy is built around trading items inside a sanctioned system rather than trading accounts. That pushes account-level transactions further underground than, say, Free Fire or Brawl Stars, where third-party marketplaces operate comparatively openly.
